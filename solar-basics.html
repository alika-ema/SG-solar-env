Solar Basics: Understanding Photovoltaic Technology (via SERIS)

Photovoltaic (or "PV" in short) is a renewable energy form which uses direct conversion of sunlight into electrical energy using devices called solar cells. As a result of the dramatic decline in prices of solar PV modules during the past years, the adoption of solar has increased considerably around the world. Hence, solar could play an important role in the global electricity generation mix in the future.

How Solar Cells Work

Almost all solar cells on the market today use semiconductor materials to harness solar energy using the photovoltaic (PV) effect. However, silicon wafer-based technologies dominate today's market. Sunlight can be considered as a flux of particles called photons, whereas electric current is a flux of free (i.e. unbound) electrons. In essence, solar cells convert the energy of a photon flux into energy of an electron flux.

Due to the high density of photons in sunlight, single solar cells generate very large electric currents (of the order of hundreds of Amperes per m²). To reduce the ohmic losses associated with such currents, solar cells are constrained in terms of size (presently to less than 300 cm²). Since solar cells generate DC voltages of less than 1 V, many cells need to be connected in series to form a module that generates a useful voltage (typically 30-40 V).

Solar Cell Performance Metrics

The performance of a solar cell is typically characterized by its efficiency, which is the ratio of electrical power output to incident solar power. Modern commercial silicon solar cells achieve efficiencies of 18-22%, while laboratory cells have reached over 26%.

Key parameters include:

  • Open-circuit voltage (Voc): The maximum voltage available from a solar cell when no current flows
  • Short-circuit current (Isc): The maximum current when the voltage is zero
  • Fill Factor (FF): A measure of the quality of the solar cell, representing the ratio of maximum power to the product of Voc and Isc
  • Efficiency (η): The overall conversion efficiency from sunlight to electricity

Types of Solar Technologies

There are several types of solar PV technologies available:

Monocrystalline Silicon

Made from single-crystal silicon, these panels offer the highest efficiency (18-22%) and longest lifespan. They perform well in low-light conditions and have a uniform black appearance.

Polycrystalline Silicon

Made from multiple silicon crystals melted together. Slightly lower efficiency (15-17%) but more affordable. They have a distinctive blue, speckled appearance.

Thin-Film

Made by depositing thin layers of photovoltaic material on a substrate. Lower efficiency (10-12%) but flexible and lightweight. Suitable for specific applications where weight or flexibility is important.

Solar in Singapore's Context

Singapore receives abundant sunlight year-round, with an average annual solar irradiance of approximately 1,580 kWh/m². This makes solar PV a viable renewable energy option for the country. The government has set a target of deploying at least 2 GWp of solar capacity by 2030, enough to power about 350,000 households annually.

For landed homeowners, installing solar panels can significantly reduce electricity bills while contributing to Singapore's sustainability goals. With various financing options including zero upfront cost models, solar adoption has become increasingly accessible.
