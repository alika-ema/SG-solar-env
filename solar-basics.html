Solar PV turns sunlight into electricity for your home or building. This guide explains the basics in simple terms to help you understand how solar works before you make a decision.

What is Solar PV?

Solar photovoltaic (PV) systems convert sunlight directly into electricity. The word "photovoltaic" comes from "photo" (light) and "voltaic" (electricity).

Solar PV vs Solar Thermal: What's the Difference?

  • Solar PV makes electricity from sunlight. This electricity powers your lights, appliances, and air conditioning.
  • Solar Thermal uses the sun's heat, typically to warm up water for showers and taps.

Solar panels generate direct current (DC) electricity. Since homes use alternating current (AC) electricity, a device called an inverter converts the DC electricity from the panels into AC electricity that your home can use.

How a Typical Solar PV System Works

A typical rooftop solar system has a few main parts working together:

☀️ PV Modules/Panels

Capture sunlight and convert it into DC electricity

⚡ Inverter

Converts DC electricity into AC electricity for your home

🔌 Electrical Distribution Board

Distributes electricity to your home's circuits and appliances

📊 Meter

Measures how much electricity you generate and use

During the day: Your solar system supplies part of your home's electricity needs. If your system produces more electricity than you need, the excess flows to the national grid.

At night: Solar panels need sunlight to work, so they don't generate electricity after sunset. Your home draws electricity from the grid as usual.

Types of Solar PV Systems

There are two main types of solar systems:

Grid-Connected Systems

These are connected to the electricity grid. This is the most common type in Singapore. You can use solar power during the day and draw from the grid at night or when clouds block the sun.

Off-Grid Systems

These operate independently from the grid and usually require batteries to store electricity for use at night. These are rarely used in Singapore since we have a reliable national grid.

In Singapore, grid-connected systems are the standard choice for landed homes because they are simpler, more cost-effective, and don't require expensive battery storage.

Common Solar Panel Technologies

Most solar panels fall into two broad categories:

Crystalline Silicon Panels

  • Most widely used technology
  • More space-efficient (generate more power per square metre)
  • Well-proven with long track records
  • Best for rooftops with limited space

Thin-Film Panels

  • Require more roof space for the same power output
  • Some types perform better in very hot conditions
  • Can be more flexible in design
  • Less common for residential rooftops in Singapore

What Affects Solar Performance?

Several factors influence how much electricity your solar system will generate:

  • Sunlight availability: More sunlight means more electricity. Singapore's location near the equator gives us good year-round sunlight.
  • Roof location and angle: Roofs facing the sun's path generate more power. In Singapore, panels are typically installed flat or with a slight tilt.
  • Shading: Trees, nearby buildings, or even chimneys can cast shadows that significantly reduce performance. Even partial shading can affect the whole system.
  • Temperature: Solar panels actually work slightly less efficiently in very hot weather, though they still produce plenty of electricity.
  • Panel cleanliness: Dust, leaves, or bird droppings can block sunlight. A slight tilt helps rainwater wash away dirt naturally.

In Singapore, panels generally perform best when installed on unshaded rooftops with good sun exposure throughout the day.

Where Solar Panels Are Usually Installed

Most solar panels in Singapore are mounted on rooftops, which typically have the best sun exposure and least shading.

Panels can also be integrated into other building elements if they receive adequate sunlight:

  • Building façades (vertical surfaces)
  • Skylights and canopy roofs
  • Car porch covers
  • External cladding or sunshades

The key is ensuring the surface receives direct sunlight for most of the day without significant shading.

Expected Energy Generation

Solar output varies depending on several factors including system size, panel technology, weather conditions, temperature, and time of day. Remember, solar panels only produce electricity when sunlight is available.

Indicative Example:

A typical 10 kW rooftop solar PV system in Singapore using crystalline modules can generate roughly 11,000–12,500 kWh per year. Some thin-film systems may produce slightly more depending on specific conditions.

Note: This is an estimate only. Actual generation depends on your specific roof conditions, equipment choice, and weather patterns.

Cost and Maintenance Basics

Solar PV systems typically have higher upfront costs but relatively low operating costs once installed. Over time, the savings on electricity bills can offset the initial investment.

Maintenance is generally minimal, but regular inspection is still important. Most experts recommend preventive maintenance every 6 to 12 months.

Common maintenance checks include:

  • Checking for dirt, dust, or debris on panels
  • Looking for physical damage or cracks
  • Inspecting electrical connections for looseness
  • Checking for corrosion on mounting structures
  • Verifying inverter performance and display readings

Many installers offer maintenance packages as part of their service. Rainwater usually keeps panels reasonably clean, but occasional professional cleaning may be beneficial.

Important Safety Note

⚠️ Always Use Qualified Professionals

Solar PV systems involve high-voltage electricity and structural modifications to your roof. They should only be designed and installed by qualified and licensed professionals.

  • Ensure proper earthing (grounding) to prevent electrical hazards
  • Verify structural safety of your roof before installation
  • Install appropriate lightning protection where needed
  • Engage a Licensed Electrical Worker (LEW) for electrical work
  • Work with a Qualified Person (QP) for structural assessments

Frequently Asked Questions

Does solar work on cloudy days?

Yes, but at reduced efficiency. Solar panels can still generate electricity from diffuse sunlight on cloudy days, typically producing 10-25% of their rated capacity depending on cloud thickness.

Does solar work at night?

No. Solar panels need sunlight to generate electricity. At night, your home will draw power from the grid unless you have a battery storage system.

What is an inverter?

An inverter is a device that converts the DC (direct current) electricity produced by solar panels into AC (alternating current) electricity that your home appliances can use. It's often called the "brain" of the solar system.

Why does shading matter?

Shading can significantly reduce system performance because solar panels are connected in series. When one part is shaded, it can affect the entire string of panels. Even small shadows from trees or chimneys can cause disproportionate power losses.

Do solar panels need maintenance?

Yes, but minimal. Panels should be inspected every 6-12 months for dirt, damage, and loose connections. In Singapore, rainwater usually keeps them reasonably clean, but occasional professional cleaning may improve performance.
